How to Choose a Dash Cam

A dash cam is cheap insurance — but only if the footage is actually usable. Prioritize resolution, reliable storage, and a parking mode, and skip the gimmicks.

By Glovebox Gear Editorial Team · Updated August 19, 2026 · Independently researched

Most dash-cam regret comes from blurry plates and cards that fill up. Here’s what actually matters.

Resolution: 1440p is the sweet spot

1080p is the floor; 1440p (2K) reads license plates more reliably without huge files. 4K looks great but eats storage fast — only worth it if you want maximum detail.

Don’t skip these

  • Parking mode — records hits while you’re parked (needs a hardwire kit for best results).
  • Supercapacitor, not battery — survives summer heat far better.
  • A high-endurance microSD — regular cards die from constant overwriting.
  • GPS — logs speed and location, useful for disputes.

Frequently asked questions

What resolution dash cam do I need?

1440p (2K) is the sweet spot — it reads license plates reliably without the huge file sizes of 4K. 1080p is the acceptable minimum.

Do I need a front and rear dash cam?

If you want to capture rear-end collisions and tailgaters — or you drive rideshare — yes. A front-only cam only protects against what’s ahead of you.

Will a dash cam drain my battery?

Not in normal driving. For parking mode, hardwire it with a kit that cuts off at a safe voltage so it won’t drain your battery overnight.
