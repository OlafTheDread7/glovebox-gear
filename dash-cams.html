What actually matters in a dash cam

Resolution gets the headlines, but a readable license plate depends just as much on the sensor and lens. A good 1440p camera often beats a cheap '4K' one. Prioritize cameras with strong low-light performance — most incidents you care about happen at dusk or night.

If you want protection while parked, you need a parking mode plus a way to keep the camera powered: either a hardwire kit tapped into your fuse box or a dedicated battery pack. Relying on the car battery alone risks a no-start.

Don't forget the memory card

Dash cams write continuously, which destroys ordinary microSD cards fast. Buy a high-endurance card (32–256GB) rated for surveillance or dash-cam use, and reformat it every few weeks to keep recordings reliable.

Frequently asked questions

Are dash cams legal?

In most US states, yes — but windshield-mounting rules vary. Many drivers mount behind the rearview mirror to stay within local size and placement laws, so check your state's regulations.

Do dash cams record when the car is off?

Only if they have a parking mode, and that requires keeping the camera powered — either through a hardwire kit wired to your fuse box or a separate battery pack.

What memory card do I need?

A high-endurance microSD card between 32GB and 256GB, rated for continuous recording. Standard cards wear out quickly under constant writing.
